Shopping in New Zealand

New Zealand offers a mix of distinctly local products, from traditional Maori craftsmanship to farm-fresh goods, making it easy to bring home something meaningful rather than generic souvenirs.

Must-Buy Items

Pounamu (greenstone/jade)
Traditional Maori carvings, often made into pendants; considered a treasured, culturally significant gift. Best bought from authentic Maori-owned carvers rather than mass-produced tourist shops.
Merino wool products
NZ is known for premium merino wool; look for sweaters, socks, and blankets from brands like Icebreaker or Untouched World.
Manuka honey
A distinctly Kiwi product with a strong reputation; look for the UMF (Unique Manuka Factor) rating on the label to ensure quality.
Sheepskin products
Rugs, slippers, and boots (like UGG-style footwear) made from NZ sheepskin — a nod to the country's farming heritage.
Kiwiana souvenirs
Fun, lighthearted items reflecting NZ pop culture: jandals (flip-flops), paua shell jewelry, or anything featuring the kiwi bird or silver fern.
NZ wine
Bottles of Marlborough Sauvignon Blanc or Central Otago Pinot Noir make for a well-regarded, easy-to-pack gift (if traveling by checked luggage).

Where to Shop

Local markets
Best for authentic, small-batch goods and fresh produce. Examples: Auckland's La Cigale Market, Christchurch Farmers Market, Nelson Market.
Maori-owned craft stores
For genuine pounamu and carved goods, especially around Rotorua and the South Island's West Coast (a traditional source of greenstone).
Wool and outdoor stores
Found in most major cities and tourist towns like Queenstown, good for merino clothing and outdoor gear.
Duty-free at airports
Convenient last-stop option for wine, honey, and packaged snacks before departure.

A Note on Authenticity

Travelers are encouraged to buy pounamu and Maori craft items from Maori-owned businesses rather than imported imitations, both to support local communities and ensure the item's cultural significance is respected.
